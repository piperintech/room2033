    Spin School

    How to set up your turntable, play a record, and keep your vinyl alive for decades.

    First setup (do this once)

    1. Find a level, solid spot. Not on top of a speaker. Vibrations and tilt make the needle skip. A cheap bubble level or a level app on your phone works.
    2. Remove the shipping bits. Take off the plastic stylus guard over the needle, any twist-tie on the tonearm, and the transport screw if the manual mentions one.
    3. Put on the platter and belt if they came separate. The manual shows how the belt loops around the motor pulley.
    4. Set the tracking force (only if your arm has a counterweight on the back). Balance the arm so it floats level, zero the dial, then turn to the grams your cartridge asks for, usually 1.5–2.5 g. Set anti-skate to the same number.
    5. Connect the sound. Turntables need a phono preamp. If yours has a PHONO/LINE switch, use LINE for powered speakers or a normal amp input, PHONO only if the amp has a "Phono" input. Wrong choice = super quiet or very loud and distorted.

    Playing a record

    1. Hold it by the edges and the label. Never touch the grooves; skin oil attracts dust.
    2. Pick the speed. Most albums are 33⅓. 7-inch singles and some audiophile pressings are 45. It's printed on the label or sleeve.
    3. Brush it. A few slow turns with a carbon-fiber brush while it spins.
    4. Cue it. Lift the cue lever, move the arm over the first groove at the outer edge, lower the lever. Let it drop slowly on its own.
    5. Turn the volume up after the needle lands, not before.
    6. At the end, lift with the cue lever, return the arm to its rest, and put the record back in its inner sleeve right away.

    Keep them alive

    • Store records standing up, like books. Stacked flat, they warp.
    • Keep them out of the sun and away from heat. A Texas car in summer will ruin a record in an hour.
    • Swap paper inner sleeves for anti-static poly-lined ones.
    • Replace the stylus roughly every 500–1,000 hours of play.
    • Close the dust cover while it plays if your deck has one.

    Starter kit (cheap, worth it)

    • Carbon-fiber record brush
    • Anti-static inner sleeves (pack of 50)
    • Outer plastic sleeves to protect the covers
    • Stylus brush
    • A cube shelf that fits 12.5-inch records (IKEA Kallax is the classic)

    Reading used-record grades

    MMint. Sealed or never played. Rare used.
    NMNear Mint. Looks and plays like new. Safe buy.
    VG+Light marks, plays clean. The sweet spot for price.
    VGSome surface noise and clicks. Fine for cheap finds.
    G / F / PNoisy, possibly skips. Skip unless it's rare.

    Where to hunt in Dallas

    • Josey Records (Dallas). Huge new and used stock.
    • Good Records on Lower Greenville.
    • Spinster Records in Oak Cliff.
    • Half Price Books. Check the used bins, all locations.
    • Record Store Day (April and Black Friday) for limited colored pressings.

    Online: Discogs (compare sellers, check feedback), eBay, the artist's official store, Target and Walmart sales.

    The ritual

    Vinyl is the opposite of shuffle. Pick one album, play side A start to finish, then get up and flip it. No phone. Lights low. The first time you hear an album this way, write one line about it in the record's notes in your crate, so the collection becomes a journal of what each record meant to you when you met it.
